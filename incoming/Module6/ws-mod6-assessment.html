    Section 6 Module 6: Assessment

    This assessment has three parts. Parts 1 and 2 ask you to write complete mathematical proofs, submitted for feedback. Part 3 is a short reflection. Submit all three parts together as a single document (PDF or typed file) through the course management system.
    🔗
    Write-up standard. All proofs must be written in complete mathematical prose. Use the style modeled in the Worked Examples: announce your strategy, include scratch work (clearly labeled) before the formal proof, write in complete grammatical sentences, invoke each definition or theorem by name, and justify every step. Work consisting only of symbolic manipulations without explanation will not receive full credit.
    🔗
    A note on Part 2. Part 2 is a single required proof problem; there are no options to choose between. All students complete the same Part 2 problem.
    🔗
    Academic integrity. Your proofs must be your own work. You may refer to your notes, the textbooks, and the worked examples from this module. You may not collaborate with other students on the written proofs or use AI writing tools to draft your arguments.
    🔗
    Part 1: An \(\varepsilon\)-\(\delta\) Limit Proof (Required) (Estimated time: 15 minutes)
    🔗

    Checkpoint 6.1. Proving a Limit from the Definition.

    Prove that \(\lim_{x \to 3} (2x + 1) = 7\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Your proof must have the following structure:
    1. Scratch work. Compute \(|(2x + 1) - 7|\) and find the value of \(\delta\) (in terms of \(\varepsilon\)) that will make the proof work. Show the algebra explicitly.
      🔗
    2. Formal proof. State “Let \(\varepsilon \gt 0\)” and “Set \(\delta = \ldots\text{.}\)” Assume \(0 \lt |x - 3| \lt \delta\) and verify the chain of inequalities concluding \(|(2x + 1) - 7| \lt \varepsilon\text{.}\)
      🔗
    3. Conclusion. Invoke the definition of limit by name.
      🔗
    🔗
    (Hint: \(|(2x + 1) - 7| = 2|x - 3|\text{;}\) set \(\delta = \varepsilon / 2\text{.}\))
    🔗
    🔗
    Part 2: Uniform Continuity (Required) (Estimated time: 20 minutes)
    🔗

    Checkpoint 6.2. Proving Uniform Continuity.

    Prove that \(f(x) = \sqrt{x}\) is uniformly continuous on \([0, \infty)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Your proof must have the following structure:
    1. Strategy. State that you will use the inequality \(|\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y}| \leq \sqrt{|x - y|}\) for all \(x, y \geq 0\text{.}\)
      🔗
    2. Verify the key inequality. Prove that \(|\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y}| \leq \sqrt{|x - y|}\) for all \(x, y \geq 0\text{.}\) (Hint: it suffices to prove that the square of the left side is at most the square of the right side when both are nonnegative. Assume without loss of generality \(x \geq y \geq 0\text{;}\) then \(|x - y| = x - y\text{,}\) and the inequality \((\sqrt{x} - \sqrt{y})^2 \leq x - y\) is equivalent to \(x - 2\sqrt{xy} + y \leq x - y\text{,}\) i.e., \(2y \leq 2\sqrt{xy}\text{,}\) i.e., \(\sqrt{y} \leq \sqrt{x}\text{,}\) which holds since \(x \geq y\text{.}\))
      🔗
    3. Formal uniform continuity proof. Given \(\varepsilon \gt 0\text{,}\) set \(\delta = \varepsilon^2\text{.}\) Suppose \(x, y \in [0, \infty)\) with \(|x - y| \lt \delta\text{.}\) Use the key inequality to conclude \(|f(x) - f(y)| \lt \varepsilon\text{,}\) and invoke the definition of uniform continuity by name.
      🔗

    Write 3–5 sentences for each question. These are graded for thoughtfulness, not for mathematical correctness.

    Checkpoint 6.3. The Sequence–Function Parallel.

    Compare the \(\varepsilon\)-\(\delta\) definition of \(\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L\) to the \(\varepsilon\)-\(N\) definition of \(\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = L\) from Module 4. What is structurally identical between the two definitions? What is genuinely different?
    🔗

    Checkpoint 6.4. Bauldry’s Presentation.

    In the Bridge Reading you compared Zorn §3.1–3.4 to Bauldry §2.2. Identify one place where Bauldry’s treatment in §2.2 is more compressed than Zorn’s. What background knowledge does Bauldry assume that Zorn develops explicitly?
    🔗

    Checkpoint 6.5. Looking Ahead to Module 8.

    Uniform continuity will appear in the proof that continuous functions are Riemann integrable (Module 8). Based on what you know now about uniform continuity, describe in two or three sentences how you expect uniform continuity to enter that argument. (A partial answer is: to approximate the integral, we need to control \(|f(x) - f(y)|\) on small subintervals; uniform continuity gives us a single \(\delta\) that works everywhere at once. Articulating this connection is the point.)
