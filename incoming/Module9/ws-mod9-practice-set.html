    Section 4 Module 9: Practice Problem Set

    Problems are labeled \([\mathbf{F}]\) (Foundational), \([\mathbf{S}]\) (Standard), and \([\mathbf{C}]\) (Challenge). Work in order within each level; later problems often build on earlier ones.
    🔗
    These problems are not submitted, but write out complete solutions. For any problem asking you to prove something, apply the standards from the Worked Examples: strategy stated up front, complete sentences, every step justified, and each theorem cited by name.
    🔗

    Foundational Problems.

    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.1. \([\mathbf{F}]\) Pointwise Limits.

    Find the pointwise limit of each sequence of functions on the given set. No proof of convergence is required; a sentence of justification is enough.
    1. \(f_n(x) = \dfrac{1}{1 + nx}\) on \((0, \infty)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(f_n(x) = \dfrac{nx}{1 + nx}\) on \([0, \infty)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. \(f_n(x) = \dfrac{\sin(nx)}{n}\) on \(\R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. \(f_n(x) = \left(1 + \dfrac{x}{n}\right)^n\) on \(\R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) For \(x \gt 0\text{:}\) \(nx \to \infty\text{,}\) so \(1/(1+nx) \to 0\text{.}\) Pointwise limit: \(f \equiv 0\) on \((0,\infty)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) At \(x = 0\text{:}\) \(f_n(0) = 0\) for all \(n\text{,}\) so \(f(0) = 0\text{.}\) For \(x \gt 0\text{:}\) \(nx/(1+nx) = 1/(1/nx + 1) \to 1\text{.}\) Pointwise limit: \(f(0)=0\text{,}\) \(f(x)=1\) for \(x \gt 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (c) \(|\sin(nx)/n| \leq 1/n \to 0\text{.}\) Pointwise limit: \(f \equiv 0\) on \(\R\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (d) Standard limit: \((1+x/n)^n \to e^x\text{.}\) Pointwise limit: \(f(x) = e^x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.2. \([\mathbf{F}]\) Sup-Norm Computations.

    For each sequence in F1, compute \(S_n = \sup_{x \in D} |f_n(x) - f(x)|\) (where \(f\) is the pointwise limit from F1) and determine whether \(S_n \to 0\text{.}\)
    1. Part (a) of F1, on \((0, \infty)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Part (b) of F1, on \([0, \infty)\) and on \([1, \infty)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Part (c) of F1, on \(\R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(|f_n(x) - 0| = 1/(1+nx)\text{;}\) \(\sup_{x \gt 0} 1/(1+nx) = 1\) (approaching as \(x \to 0^+\)). \(S_n = 1\) for all \(n\text{.}\) Not uniform on \((0,\infty)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) On \([0,\infty)\text{:}\) at \(x=0\text{,}\) \(|f_n(0)-0|=0\text{;}\) for \(x \gt 0\text{,}\) \(|f_n(x)-1| = 1/(1+nx) \leq 1\text{,}\) approaching \(1\) as \(x \to 0^+\text{.}\) So \(S_n = 1\text{.}\) Not uniform. On \([1,\infty)\text{:}\) \(\sup_{x \geq 1} 1/(1+nx) = 1/(1+n) \to 0\text{.}\) Uniform on \([1,\infty)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (c) \(S_n = \sup_\R |\sin(nx)/n| = 1/n \to 0\text{.}\) Uniform on \(\R\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.3. \([\mathbf{F}]\) Uniform Convergence: Quick Checks.

    For each, state whether the convergence is uniform on the given set, and give a one-sentence justification.
    1. \(f_n(x) = x/n\) on \([0,1]\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(f_n(x) = x/n\) on \([0, \infty)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. \(f_n(x) = x^2/(x^2 + n)\) on \(\R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. \(f_n(x) = e^{-nx}\) on \((0, \infty)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(\sup_{[0,1]} |x/n| = 1/n \to 0\text{.}\) Uniform.
    🔗
    (b) \(\sup_{[0,\infty)} |x/n| = \infty\) for every \(n\) (unbounded domain). Not uniform.
    🔗
    (c) \(|x^2/(x^2+n)| \leq x^2/n\text{;}\) but \(x^2/n\) is unbounded on \(\R\text{.}\) More directly: \(\sup_{x \in \R} x^2/(x^2+n) = 1\) for all \(n\) (limit as \(x \to \infty\)). Not uniform on \(\R\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (d) Pointwise limit is \(0\text{.}\) \(\sup_{x \gt 0} e^{-nx} = 1\) (as \(x \to 0^+\)). Not uniform on \((0,\infty)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.4. \([\mathbf{F}]\) \(M\)-Test Warm-Up.

    Apply the Weierstrass \(M\)-Test to each series. State the bound \(M_n\) and verify \(\sum M_n \lt \infty\text{.}\)
    1. \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n!}\) on \([-5,5]\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2 + x^2}\) on \(\R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^n\) on \([-1,1]\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(M_n = 5^n/n!\text{;}\) \(\sum 5^n/n! = e^5 \lt \infty\text{.}\) Uniform on \([-5,5]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (b) \(1/(n^2+x^2) \leq 1/n^2 =: M_n\) for all \(x \in \R\text{;}\) \(\sum 1/n^2 \lt \infty\text{.}\) Uniform on \(\R\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (c) \(|(x/2)^n| \leq (1/2)^n =: M_n\) on \([-1,1]\text{;}\) \(\sum (1/2)^n = 1 \lt \infty\text{.}\) Uniform on \([-1,1]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Standard Problems.

    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.5. \([\mathbf{S}]\) Proving Uniform Convergence.

    Prove that \(f_n(x) = x^n/n\) converges uniformly to \(0\) on \([0,1]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Your proof must: (i) identify the pointwise limit; (ii) compute \(\sup_{x \in [0,1]} |f_n(x) - 0|\) exactly; (iii) show this sup tends to \(0\) and invoke the sup-norm criterion.
    🔗
    Hint.
    On \([0,1]\text{,}\) \(x^n \leq 1\text{,}\) so \(\sup_{[0,1]} x^n/n \leq 1/n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.6. \([\mathbf{S}]\) Non-Uniform: Using a Sequence.

    Prove that \(f_n(x) = nx\,e^{-nx}\) does not converge uniformly to \(0\) on \((0, \infty)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Your proof must exhibit a sequence of points \(x_n \in (0,\infty)\) such that \(|f_n(x_n) - 0| \not\to 0\text{,}\) thereby showing \(\sup_{(0,\infty)} |f_n(x)| \not\to 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    Find the maximizer of \(nx\,e^{-nx}\) by taking the derivative in \(x\) and setting it to zero. The max is at \(x = 1/n\) with value \(e^{-1}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.7. \([\mathbf{S}]\) Term-by-Term Integration.

    Let \(f_n(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k x^{2k}\) be the \(n\)th partial sum of the geometric series for \(1/(1+x^2)\text{.}\)
    1. Show that \(f_n \to 1/(1+x^2)\) uniformly on \([-r,r]\) for any \(0 \lt r \lt 1\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Use term-by-term integration to compute \(\int_0^x f_n(t)\,dt\) and take the limit as \(n \to \infty\) to recover the formula \(\arctan(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k x^{2k+1}}{2k+1}\) for \(|x| \lt 1\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.8. \([\mathbf{S}]\) Radius of Convergence and Uniform Convergence.

    Find the radius of convergence of each power series using the Ratio Test. Then for each, state the largest set on which the \(M\)-Test guarantees uniform convergence.
    1. \(\displaystyle \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n \cdot 3^n}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. \(\displaystyle \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n!\,x^n\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. \(\displaystyle \displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^n}{n^2 + 1}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) Ratio \(\to |x|/3\text{.}\) \(R = 3\text{.}\) Uniform on \([-r,r]\) for any \(r \lt 3\) (by \(M\)-Test with \(M_n = r^n/(n \cdot 3^n)\)).
    🔗
    (b) Ratio \(= (n+1)|x| \to \infty\) for any \(x \neq 0\text{.}\) \(R = 0\text{:}\) the series converges only at \(x = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (c) Ratio \(\to |x|\text{.}\) \(R = 1\text{.}\) Uniform on \([-r,r]\) for any \(r \lt 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.9. \([\mathbf{S}]\) Uniform Convergence and Differentiation.

    Let \(f_n(x) = \sin(nx)/\sqrt{n}\) on \(\R\text{.}\)
    1. Show that \(f_n \to 0\) uniformly on \(\R\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Compute \(f_n'(x)\text{.}\) Does \(f_n'(x)\) converge pointwise to \(0\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. What does this example show about whether uniform convergence of \((f_n)\) implies convergence of \((f_n')\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    (a) \(\sup_\R |\sin(nx)/\sqrt{n}| \leq 1/\sqrt{n} \to 0\text{.}\) Uniform.
    🔗
    (b) \(f_n'(x) = \sqrt{n}\cos(nx)\text{.}\) At \(x = 0\text{:}\) \(f_n'(0) = \sqrt{n} \to \infty\text{.}\) So \(f_n'\) does not converge to \(0\) pointwise at \(x = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (c) Uniform convergence of \((f_n)\) does NOT imply convergence of the derivatives \((f_n')\text{.}\) Differentiating term by term requires an additional condition (e.g., uniform convergence of the derivatives; this is a theorem for MAT 5610).
    🔗

    Challenge Problems.

    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.10. \([\mathbf{C}]\) Proof of the Term-by-Term Integration Theorem.

    Prove: if \(f_n \to f\) uniformly on \([a,b]\) and each \(f_n\) is Riemann integrable on \([a,b]\text{,}\) then \(f\) is Riemann integrable and \(\int_a^b f = \lim_{n \to \infty} \int_a^b f_n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Your proof must use the following steps: (i) show \(f\) is bounded (since each \(f_n\) is bounded and convergence is uniform); (ii) estimate \(\left|\int_a^b f_n - \int_a^b f\right| \leq (b-a) \cdot \sup_x |f_n(x) - f(x)|\text{;}\) (iii) conclude by the definition of limit.
    🔗
    Hint.
    For step (ii), use \(\left|\int_a^b (f_n - f)\right| \leq \int_a^b |f_n - f| \leq (b-a) \cdot \sup_x |f_n(x) - f(x)|\text{.}\) For step (i): there exists \(N\) such that \(|f_n(x) - f(x)| \lt 1\) for all \(n \geq N\) and \(x \in [a,b]\text{;}\) so \(|f(x)| \leq |f_N(x)| + 1 \leq M + 1\) where \(M\) bounds \(f_N\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 4.11. \([\mathbf{C}]\) Uniform Convergence of a Power Series.

    Let \(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n x^n\) be a power series with radius of convergence \(R \gt 0\text{.}\) Prove that for any \(0 \lt r \lt R\text{,}\) the series converges uniformly on \([-r, r]\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Your proof must: (i) observe that \(|a_n x^n| \leq |a_n| r^n\) for \(x \in [-r,r]\text{;}\) (ii) show \(\sum |a_n| r^n \lt \infty\) (use the definition of radius of convergence: the series \(\sum a_n r^n\) converges absolutely because \(r \lt R\)); (iii) apply the Weierstrass \(M\)-Test with \(M_n = |a_n| r^n\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Hint.
    The key fact: since \(r \lt R\text{,}\) we can pick \(r'\) with \(r \lt r' \lt R\) such that \(\sum |a_n| (r')^n \lt \infty\text{.}\) Then \(|a_n| r^n = |a_n| (r')^n \cdot (r/r')^n \leq C (r/r')^n\) for some \(C\text{,}\) and \(\sum (r/r')^n \lt \infty\text{.}\)
